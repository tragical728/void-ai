How a build runs

Four weeks from map to live

The order matters here: we do not touch a tool until we have timed the work by hand, and no agent talks to a customer until it has spent a week proving itself in silence.

01

Map

Week 1

We sit with the people doing the work and time it. What comes out is a short list of tasks ranked by hours lost and how safely they can be handed over.

02

Build

Weeks 2–3

Agents wired into the tools you already pay for. Your rules, your tone, your escalation paths. Every decision the agent makes is logged in plain language.

03

Shadow

Week 4

The agent runs on live work but nothing it produces is sent. Your team reads its output next to their own and corrects it until the two are indistinguishable.

04

Watch

Ongoing

It goes live with a kill switch and a weekly report. We tune what drifts. You keep the build — the agents, the prompts, the code — whatever happens to us.

Before you ask

The three questions everyone has

What happens when the agent gets it wrong?+

It escalates instead of improvising. Every agent ships with explicit limits — what it may decide alone, what needs a human, what it must never touch. It runs a week in shadow mode before anyone outside the company sees its output, every action is logged in plain language, and there is a kill switch that stops it in one click.

Where does our data go?+

Into your own accounts. We build inside your cloud and your tools, with the region you require. Your content is not used to train anything, retention is set by you, and access is scoped per agent — the invoicing agent cannot read your sales inbox.

What does it cost, and when does it pay for itself?+

A build is a fixed price agreed after the mapping week, plus a monthly fee for running and tuning it. Most builds return their cost inside the first quarter — the calculator above is the same arithmetic we use to decide whether a task is worth automating at all. If the numbers do not work, we say so and we do not take the project.
